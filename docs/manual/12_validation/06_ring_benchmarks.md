# Ring-mode benchmarks

Ring mode's element maps and optics against independent references: MAD-X
5.09 (`TWISS` and its exact-model `PTC_TWISS`), two reference codes, and
analytic results.  Reference code A tracks with element maps whose bend
multipoles are expanded in Cartesian coordinates; reference code B with
exact elements whose bend multipoles are curvilinear; reference code C, used
for the stripping foil and for painting, tracks thick elements and scatters
particles in a foil one screened-Rutherford collision at a time.  Every
reference is generated by a script that never imports HELIX and is stored
with a manifest (the SHA-256 of the data — of the inputs too for some — and
the options) in `tests/ring/fixtures/refs/`; the tests in `tests/ring/` check
against the stored files, so they run without the reference codes.

The test ring is the simplified Fermilab Booster (24 cells, 474.2 m,
combined-function bends with a sextupole component, pole faces of half the
bend angle, 800 MeV protons), a public deck under a BSD licence
(`tests/ring/fixtures/`).

## Element maps against reference code A

One set of 64 particles (|x|, |y| ≤ 3 mm, |δ| ≤ 2×10⁻³, |ζ| ≤ 0.3 m) through 24
single elements of reference code A: drifts, quadrupoles, sextupole,
octupole, a thin multipole up to order 4 (normal and skew), combined-function
bends in each of its bend models and integrators, with a field error, with
linear and full pole faces at both bend signs, a straight bend, a cavity,
frame rotations and shifts, a wedge and a linear edge.

| Agreement | Cases |
|---|---|
| 10⁻¹³ relative (10⁻¹⁴ m in ζ, a sum of path-length differences) | 21 |
| Set by rounding: the exact curved-bend formula, which rounds in both codes (each about 1.5×10⁻¹³ m from a 40-digit evaluation; 1.4×10⁻¹³ m apart); the reference's straight bend (10⁻¹¹ relative) and δ after a cavity (5×10⁻¹⁶), which HELIX evaluates without cancellation | 3 |

The plain-Python and compiled kernels give identical results bit for bit.

## Symplecticity

The Jacobian of every node's map in canonical coordinates, by central
differences in 50-digit arithmetic, satisfies |JᵀSJ − S| ≤ 2×10⁻¹⁶ for all 31
maps tested (the largest values come from sines and cosines stored as
float64 parameters); a deliberately non-symplectic map (a kick that depends
on px) shows 3.6×10⁻⁴ — the check sees it.

## Thin-lens FODO ring

Twelve cells of thin quadrupoles (f = 4 m) and 3 m drifts: tunes (with the
integer part), β and α at the start equal the analytic values to 10⁻¹², and
the chromaticity equals the thin-lens sum −(1/4π) Σ β k to 10⁻⁶.

## Against HELIX's own linear matrices

Every element of the Booster (its RF cavity at its own voltage), and
vertical and negative bends, tilted, skew and shifted magnets, thin lenses and
steerers, for a proton lattice and an H⁻ one: the ring nodes' Jacobian at the
reference (50-digit arithmetic) equals HELIX's linac element matrix to 10⁻¹⁰.
The whole turn agrees to 2×10⁻⁸, the floor of a float64 finite difference.
Two differences are by design, and what both describe is compared:

* an off-centre magnet also makes dispersion through its chromatic focusing,
  which the linac matrix leaves to the orbit — its transverse block is
  compared, and its dispersion terms are checked against the closed form;
* a thin ring cavity has no transverse map (as MAD-X's); the linac gap adds
  its transverse RF defocusing (10⁻⁶ /m at the Booster's 9 kV) — the
  longitudinal rows are compared.

A pole face at zero angle with a gap acts through its fringe field (a weak
defocusing across the bend plane, MAD-X's −h tan(−ψ)) in ring mode and in the
linac's edge alike: the two element matrices agree to 10⁻¹⁰ there too.  Every
edge model reproduces MAD-X's tunes for the Booster with sector faces, a 10 cm
gap and FINT 0.5 (Qy 6.6707484).

## Against reference code B

The simple Booster as reference code B runs it, with the curvilinear model:
CODATA 2022 constants (the masses formed from their values in kilograms), the
reference's integration (sixth order, six steps per magnet), its RF frequency
and voltage.

**1000 particles, two turns** (|x| up to 15 mm, |δ| up to 4.1×10⁻³), the
largest difference over all particles after the second turn:

| x | y | c t | px | py | pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.6×10⁻¹² m | 8.6×10⁻¹⁵ m | 8.6×10⁻¹³ m | 1.5×10⁻¹³ | 2.2×10⁻¹⁵ | 1.4×10⁻¹⁶ |

This is the reference's own floating-point rounding: against a 40-digit
evaluation of the same maps, the reference is off by about 3×10⁻¹² m in x
after one turn and HELIX by about 10⁻¹⁶ m.  Part of it comes from the form of
the reference's sector bend and curvilinear kicks, which subtract terms of
the order of the bending radius (HELIX evaluates the same expressions
rearranged); the rest is the rounding of its compiled code (fused
multiply-adds, its math library).  The difference has no structure in the
particles' coordinates: a cubic polynomial in the six initial coordinates
explains no more of it than it would of noise.

Single elements: the quadrupoles, the multipole and the linear edge with a
gap agree to the last bits (3×10⁻¹⁷ m in c t); the exact drift to
1.1×10⁻¹⁵ m in c t (both codes round there, each within 8×10⁻¹⁶ m of the
40-digit value).  The reference's sector bend rounds: 2.5×10⁻¹⁴ m in x with a
sextupole at negative curvature, 3×10⁻¹⁰ m with octupole and decapole
components, where HELIX is within 2×10⁻¹⁸ m of the 40-digit result.  Running
the reference's published settings (sextupoles at fourth order, five steps)
instead of the parity settings moves its particles by 4×10⁻¹² m.

**Optics.**  The reference's tunes and chromaticity from its own tracking,
against the same model's optics in HELIX:

| | Qx (fraction) | Qy (fraction) | Q′x | Q′y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| reference code B | 0.70999676 | 0.80999779 | −7.9983 | −7.9987 |
| HELIX, curvilinear model | 0.70999675 | 0.80999779 | −7.9983 | −7.9987 |

**Why the curvilinear chromaticity is not MAD-X's.**  Two differences, each
measured: linear pole faces (Cartesian bends with linear faces: −7.0144 /
−7.8338 against −8.6531 / −8.0892 with exact ones), and the curvilinear
quadrupole field inside the bends, which differs from the Cartesian field, to
the first order beyond it (cubic terms of the Hamiltonian), by a normal
sextupole −h·k1.  Cartesian bends with linear faces and that sextupole added
give −7.9983 / −7.9987, the curvilinear chromaticity (the curvilinear model
gives −7.99827 / −7.99872 with six integration steps per bend and with
sixty).

## Backends

The compiled kernels on one CPU thread and on all of them agree bit for bit
over three Booster turns of 256 particles, and the plain Python (ring mode
without numba) agrees with them bit for bit over one turn.  A particle that
leaves a map's domain (the square root of a negative number in a drift, say)
is lost as invalid at the same node, with the same coordinates, on every CPU
backend.  The CUDA kernel, run in numba's CUDA simulator on a machine without
a GPU, agrees with the CPU to 10⁻¹⁵ relative; on a GPU,
`scripts/verify_cuda_ring.py` makes the comparison ([GPU runs](../17_ring/08_gpu.md)
— it has not yet run on one).
