# HELIX 1.24.0 — consistent clocks, starting beams and surrogates across modes

Released 2026-11-10.  Every mode now changes the clock as a `FREQ` card
does, at the card or at a cavity of another frequency; starts from the
beam a multiparticle run starts from, read from a `.dst` file or
generated with an input dispersion; and tracks an element as it stands
after an edit, an error draw or a failure.  TraceWin's energy and
phase-error cards now do what TraceWin's manual says, surrogate
weights from a file answer only within accuracy limits of their native
element, and failure compensation stays within the failed element's
family.  Results change; the next section lists where.  This release
also carries the change of 1.23.1, which was not published separately:
`VaneRFQ` is retired.

## Changes that can change results

### Elements edited after they are built

* **An `NCELLS` cavity edited after it was built now changes.**  Its cells, gaps and length
  were derived once, when the cavity was built, so an edit of its field, βg, cell count,
  mode, field or gap-position corrections, frequency or transit-time table, from a Parameter
  Scan, the assistant, a study or the Python API, left the cavity as it was: a scan of the
  field gave one energy at every point (19.324096 MeV after an edit from 1 to 1.1 MV/m on
  one cavity, where the cavity built at 1.1 MV/m gives 19.356202).  An edit now derives the
  cavity again, so it is the cavity built with the new value, in every mode.  Its length
  and its transit-time βs follow from the others and can no longer be written, and the
  Parameter Scan no longer offers them; a cell count or mode must be a whole number; a step
  count left at its default follows the cell count.  A cavity with βg ≤ 0, whose cells
  follow the beam, now builds them when the beam arrives, before its slices are counted:
  its space-charge kicks were spread over a provisional length, 4.5 times too long for a
  19 MeV proton.  Above β = 0.9 that provisional length was too short instead, and every
  mode cut the end off the cavity: 133 mm of 2.62 m for a 2 GeV proton.  Cavities that are
  not edited after loading give the same results as before.

* **A bend edited after it was built has the arc of its new angle or radius.**  Its arc
  length, |ρ|·|θ|, was set when the bend was built, so after an edit of the angle or the
  radius (in the inspector, a Parameter Scan, `ADJUST`, the assistant or the Python API)
  its map took the new arc while the positions along the line, the reference phase, the
  space-charge slices, a rolled bend's steering and the MAD-X export kept the old one:
  after an edit from 10° to 11.03° at 800 mm radius, everything after the bend sat 14.4 mm
  too early.  The length now follows from the angle and the radius, and while the bend has
  an angle it can no longer be written: edit the angle or the radius (the inspector and the
  Add Element dialog let it be written only while the bend has no angle, and the Parameter
  Scan and the assistant's scan no longer offer it).  A bend set to angle 0
  has no arc, so no length: to switch a magnet off at its length, set its field error to −1
  (`field_rel = -1`).  A bend with no angle is a field-free pipe that can be given a length,
  which it keeps through rewrites of its radius and gets back when an angle given to it is
  taken away.  Edits of the angle and the radius give the same bend in any order, and undo,
  redo and the rollback of an assistant tuning plan put back exactly the values they
  changed.  A TraceWin save writes a pipe as the drift it is (a `BEND` card's length is
  ρ·θ, so the pipe's length used to be lost).  Pole faces that an importer made separate
  elements (TraceWin `EDGE` cards; MAD-X, MAD8, elegant, Bmad, SciBmad and PALS face angles)
  keep their own radius and angles, and the first edit of their bend now warns that they
  did not change.

* **The gradient matcher varies a bend's angle as `least_squares` does.**  An `ADJUST` on a
  dipole angle means the angle at the bend's radius, its arc following, in the GUI, in
  scans, in `least_squares` and in the other algorithms.  The `gradient` algorithm, the
  differentiable lattice (`set_tunables` with `"angle"`) and the differentiable step
  tracker varied the field of a magnet of fixed length instead, so their residuals and
  derivatives were those of another knob: matched to the β_x an angle of 24° gives,
  `least_squares` found 24° and `gradient` stopped at 26.01°.  The knob is now the angle;
  a field change at fixed length is `field_rel`.  With space charge the step tracker
  spreads the kicks over the knob's own arc.

* **An edit of several parameters at once is all or nothing.**  When an element refuses a
  value (such as an `NCELLS` length or half a cell), the parameters changed before it in
  the same edit go back: an assistant tuning plan used to leave them changed, with nothing
  to undo.  In the GUI the assistant now reports the element's reason, where it said the
  GUI was busy.  On the command line, in scans and in studies, a fractional value for a
  whole-number parameter is refused; it used to be truncated (6.5 cells became 6).

### Energy and phase cards

* **`SET_BEAM_ENERGY` and `SET_BEAM_E0_P0` are matching criteria, as in TraceWin.**  The
  TraceWin manual defines `SET_BEAM_ENERGY k Ei` as the criterion k(E − Ei)² and
  `SET_BEAM_E0_P0 k dE dφ ke kp` as one on the beam's energy and phase against the
  linac's; neither changes the beam.  HELIX set or shifted the reference energy with
  them, so a TraceWin deck that used `SET_BEAM_ENERGY 1 120` as a target had its energy
  forced to 120 MeV.  They now change nothing in tracking, and the matcher uses them:
  the beam's energy at the card (the reference particle's plus the bunch centroid's
  offset) against Ei, and the centroid's energy and phase offset against dE and dφ
  (the reference particle goes through the elements with their errors, so the
  second does not see an element error).  Each residual is weighted by k, as for
  the other `SET_*` cards; the `gradient` algorithm refuses them.  The energy change
  is a HELIX card now, written as a comment that TraceWin skips: `; HELIX_SET_ENERGY E`
  sets the reference energy, `; HELIX_SHIFT_ENERGY dE` shifts it, in every linac mode,
  and the Lattice tab's "E-set" adds it.  A deck that carries the TraceWin cards gets
  one notice, and each `SET_BEAM_ENERGY` with k = 0 — how HELIX wrote its setter —
  is named with the card to use instead (a strict parse refuses the deck).

* **The energy cards act in matrix mode** (`; HELIX_SET_ENERGY` and `; HELIX_SHIFT_ENERGY`,
  above), and in the phase-advance and dispersion walks, which left the reference at the old
  energy.  The walks now give the energy and phase of a multiparticle run, and M Σ₀ Mᵀ equals the
  envelope's Σ.  A MAD-X export holds one reference energy, so it keeps these cards as comments,
  with a warning.  The curved-cluster trace also treats them as a change of energy.

* **What runs after a card starts from what the line hands it.**  The period passes (matched Σ,
  channel phase advance, tune footprint) applied the cards of the prefix again on a reference that
  held them, so an energy shift counted twice and an energy card before a cavity undid its gain.
  The space-charge matched input ran its cell at the lattice's entrance reference whatever stood
  in front, and an RFQ's matched input took an energy card upstream for acceleration, with an
  emittance 18 % too small on one test.

* **`SET_BEAM_PHASE_ERROR` shifts the RF phase of the cavities after it,** as the TraceWin manual
  defines it; it did nothing in any mode (its offset went to a place no cavity read), and
  `RandomFlag` 1 raised in the multiparticle and envelope modes.  With `Dp`, every following
  cavity's beam sees its phase `Dp` later; with `RandomFlag`, a draw within ±`Dp` that an error
  study makes per seed, from a stream of its own so the other draws stay as they were (a plain run
  shifts nothing and says so).  `Dp = 0` starts the cavities after it afresh: the shifts before it
  end, and in an error study the cavities that see the arrival (field maps with `p_flag` 0,
  `NCELLS` with P = 1, a gap held at a pinned clock) take the seed's correction, the phase error
  its errors gave the beam there; a seed without errors gets exactly none.  A seed's draw and
  correction stay with it through pulse jitter and a reliability campaign, and pulse jitter is
  never corrected.  The beam's own phase is not touched, the shift follows a clock change as a
  time, the backtracker undoes it, the MAD-X export writes a gap at the phase it fires at, and the
  beam-loading model derives a cavity's voltage at that phase.

### Changes of clock and the reference phase

* **A cavity that switches the clock itself now does what a `FREQ` card does.**  An RF
  element at another frequency than the clock, with no `FREQ` card in front of it, changed
  the clock on entry without moving the reference phase, which is ω·t in degrees of the
  clock.  An `NCELLS` at `P=1` then read the beam as arriving at the wrong time (+0.047 MeV
  against +0.521 MeV behind the card, in one case), and every such cavity left the reference
  phase wrong downstream.  All RF elements now switch the clock as the card does, in every
  mode.  An LLRF frequency offset (`frequency_offset`) is not a change of clock and keeps
  its meaning: such a cavity beats against the clock from its own entrance.  A surrogate
  asked at another clock reads its phase on the cavity's clock, and a newly trained one
  records its training phase there.  Linac decks with a `FREQ` card in front of each change,
  which is every example deck, are unchanged; in the ring examples, whose cavities take
  their frequency from `HARMON`, the reference phase moves by about 1e-4°.

* **An `NCELLS` at another frequency with no `FREQ` card** made no jump in its matrix, so
  that line's z block differed from the envelope's and the tracker's.  It now holds the
  jump, as the native cavity makes it on entry.

* **The multiparticle backtrack undoes the clock switch a cavity makes itself.**  Behind an
  `NCELLS` at another frequency than the beam's, with no `FREQ` card, the backtracked phases
  came out a factor of 2 off.  They now match the same cavity behind a card.

* **A `FREQ` card now acts in matrix mode.**  Matrix mode and the phase-advance and
  dispersion walks treated the card as passive: the reference kept the old frequency
  up to the next RF element and its phase was never converted, so a multi-frequency
  line ended at 573 046° where every other mode reads 802 340°.  They now apply the
  card, and a transfer matrix across it has f₂/f₁ in its Δφ–Δφ element.  On a line whose
  frequency changes at cards, the reference equals a multiparticle run's at every
  element boundary and M Σ₀ Mᵀ equals the envelope's Σ to round-off.  What moves:
  * A whole line with field-map cavities hardly does: x and y by 1e-10, z by 2e-7,
    energy by 3e-10 MeV.
  * A line with no RF after the card: that element is f₂/f₁ instead of 1.
  * A window or period after a card now starts on the new clock.  The first cavity
    after a card used to make the jump inside the window, so its longitudinal
    determinant was f₂/f₁ and is now 1: μ_z 87.5° → 84.2° and β_z 81 → 115 on the
    first period after a card on one deck.
  * A deck with NCELLS cavities moves, because an NCELLS matrix holds no jump: on one,
    x and y by up to 1.1 %, energy by 31 keV inside the line and 0.12 keV at its exit,
    and x and y now match the tracker to 1e-10.  Compare its z block with the envelope,
    not the tracker, which adds NCELLS's synchronism factor by design.
  * `backtrack_envelope` now closes across a card; the entrance σ_φ used to come back
    wrong.
  * The transfer-matrix window's TraceWin basis now converts each side of the window
    at its own energy and clock (it used the lattice entrance's on both).  The torch
    mirror and `export --linearize` follow the same walk.

  Commands the walks still do not apply are listed under the manual's known limitations.

* **The longitudinal tune of a window that holds a change of clock.**  Across a `FREQ`
  card or a cavity at another frequency, the window's map has its exit in degrees of
  another clock, which the eigen-analysis read as part of the dynamics: a cell's μ_z came
  out 12.8° where its own clock gives 20.8°, and its matched Σ did not converge.  The
  structure tune, the channel tunes and the matched period Σ now take the window on its
  entrance clock (`clock_mhz` in the result), so μ_z is the cell's own.  What is read
  across such a window or period uses one clock too: the beam's mismatch (BMAG) across it
  and the tune footprint, whose particles start every turn on the entrance clock.  A window
  that holds no change of clock gives the same results as before.

* **An RFQ_CELL card's `dP` no longer moves the reference clock.**  Envelope,
  matrix and backtracking runs added it to the reference phase at each cell exit;
  a multiparticle run never did, so the reported phase at an RFQ exit differed by
  the sum of the column: 569° on one deck, and 10° on a TraceWin 2.27 deck, where
  the value is not a phase at all.  They now agree to about 0.5°.  Beam results are
  unchanged: inside an RFQ the phase is local to each cell, and it would only
  matter for an RF element after the RFQ that uses the absolute clock.  The value
  stays on the element, so a deck round-trips.

* **A negative drift now steps the reference clock back in every mode.**  A
  transfer-line deck can overlap two elements with one.  A multiparticle run
  stepped `s` and the phase back through it; envelope, matrix and phase-advance
  runs moved `s` only, and the backtracking table neither.  On such a deck with no
  RF after the drift, only the reported phase changes, and the backtracking table's
  `s`.  An RF element after it now sees the same clock in those modes as in a
  multiparticle run.  TraceWin's manual does not state a convention; this is the
  one consistent with `s` and the transfer matrix, which already used the signed
  length.  A reliability campaign with `phase_out` targets that started before this
  change should be started again rather than resumed: its stored baseline phase
  comes from the old clock.

### Matrix elements and the MAD-X export

* **MAD-X linearised maps after a frequency change.**  `export --linearize` converted
  the phase of every map with the line's first clock, so after a `FREQ` card, or in a map
  that makes its own jump, the longitudinal terms were off by the ratio: at a jump by 2,
  MAD-X's `TWISS` read R65 ×0.50, R56 ×1.49 and R55 ×0.97.  Each side of a map now
  converts with the clock at its point, and `TWISS` gives the line's map in matrix mode, to
  2e-4 with an energy gain, where MAD-X's drifts read the orbit's `pt`.  An `NCELLS` map
  carries TraceWin's matrix-engine synchronism factor where it acts, which the multiparticle
  tracker does not apply; the export says so for each such cavity.  A `FREQ` card stays a comment,
  because it is nothing in MAD-X's coordinates.  Exports on one clock are byte-identical.
  Ring mode no longer refuses a transverse `MATRIX` over a rounding in its z diagonal.

* **Backtracking through an explicit matrix element (`MatrixElement`, from a MAD-X `MATRIX` or
  an elegant matrix) undoes it once.**  It undid it four times, and never its centroid
  offset, so forward-then-back missed the starting beam (by 7 to 32 in particle
  coordinates on a test line), a thin element included.

* **The envelope applies an explicit matrix element once** with sub-step recording and
  with space charge, then one space-charge kick over its length, as the multiparticle
  tracker does; it applied the whole map at every slice (six times over 120 mm without
  space charge, twelve with it).  Its centroid now takes the element's offset, as the
  particles do, and with sub-step recording the multiparticle run records the element's
  exit row (it recorded none, so the rows of every later element were one off).

* **An imported MAD-X `MATRIX` follows the clock it is run at.**  MAD-X's `t` holds no
  clock, so the import converts the map's phase terms in degrees of the clock at its place
  (a cavity's frequency, or a 352.21 MHz placeholder in a line with none); run at another
  frequency they were off by the ratio (×2.17 at 162.5 MHz).  The element now records
  that clock and gives the same map in the run's degrees, in the multiparticle, envelope,
  matrix and backtracking walks.

* **A linearised MAD-X export of an accelerating line comes back with its energies.**  MAD-X keeps
  one reference momentum, so a map's gain was written as `kick6` and read back as an energy offset
  of every particle: HELIX's reference stayed at the `BEAM` energy, every element after the first
  cavity ran at the wrong energy (a section of spoke cavities, 11.4 → 20.5 MeV, came back ending
  at 11.4 MeV), and the offsets piled up across maps.  Each map is now written about HELIX's
  reference orbit in MAD-X's coordinates and read back about the same orbit: the gain is the
  reference's (`MatrixElement.ref_gain`, followed by every walk), and the line read back is the
  original, energies and maps, except that an explicit matrix element's energy offset comes back
  as the reference's gain.  MAD-X `TRACK` of the reference ends on the orbit, and a `MATRIX` deck
  from elsewhere reads as `TRACK` tracks it, where no bend or `RFCAVITY` follows a gain (MAD-X
  ties those to its fixed momentum; the export does not rescale them).  An RF gap is written as it
  fires, its own errors in. A MAD-8 deck, whose reference follows its cavities, keeps a `kick6` as
  an energy offset. The warning for a large gain now estimates MAD-X `TWISS`'s error from `pt/β₀`.

* **The MAD-X export leaves the lattice as it was.**  It walked the reference on the
  lattice it was given.  A linearised export reset its field maps and left a curved
  field-map cluster (`SUPERPOSE_MAP_OUT`) at the length it traced, so what the next export
  wrote depended on what came before (on one LEBT deck, 463.5 mm before a linearised
  export and 497.5 mm after it).  An `NCELLS` with βg ≤ 0, which builds its cells for its
  beam, was written at a provisional length 2.5 m long until a run had sized it.  The
  walk now runs on a copy.  Both elements are written with the length a run gives them,
  for the beam that reaches them (energy cards applied), plain or linearised.  Where no
  beam reaches them, they are written with their length before any run, with a warning.

### The starting beam

* **Importing a `.dst` in the Beam tab** now ticks **Continuous beam** when the
  file holds a DC beam (rms phase above 90°, an RFQ entrance); it used to clear
  the box, so such a run used bunched space charge.  The status line says when
  particles were dropped as lost, and when particles far from the median energy,
  such as those an RFQ did not accelerate, dominate σ_W and ε_z.

* **A beam read from a `.dst` file starts every mode where a multiparticle run starts
  it.**  The multiparticle run takes the file's energy, frequency, emittances and Twiss
  (the file wins, as in TraceWin); the GUI's envelope run and the CLI's envelope,
  matrix, export, Twiss, backtrack, error and failure modes took the project's, so
  modes of one project started from different beams.  They now all start from the
  file, its particles' centroid included and no mismatch applied, as a multiparticle
  run takes them, through `factory.reference_config`, which leaves the project's beam
  as it is.  So do the Surrogates tab, the Parameter Scan's envelope runs, the envelope
  drawn over a multiparticle result, the dispersion model, the structure phase advance
  of the Results popups and the Matching tab, the transfer-matrix dialog and the MAD-X
  export's message.

* **An envelope run starts from the beam's input dispersion.**  The dispersion the Beam tab sets
  (D_x, D_x', D_y, D_y'), which the multiparticle generator applies to the particles it makes,
  was left out by the GUI's envelope run and the Results tab's envelope companion, by the CLI
  and everything that runs the envelope through it (the assistant, studies, scans, reliability
  campaigns, failure compensation, the envelope backtrack), by the matching engine's envelope
  cost, the gradient matcher, grad_sensitivities and the Surrogates tab's compare.  Each now
  starts from the beam the generator makes, q = q_β + D_q·ΔW; a beam read from a file takes
  none, as before.  The starting Σ also gains the correlation of φ with the transverse
  coordinates that such a bunch carries when its α_z is not zero.  Of the example projects only
  those that set a dispersion change: on a booster-ring deck the exit σ_x of the envelope was
  8.4 % off a multiparticle run of the same beam and is now 0.34 % off (it moves by up to 27 %
  along the line); σ_y and σ_φ move by at most 0.012 %, and the others give the same results,
  bit for bit.  A continuous beam's envelope holds no energy spread before the first RF element,
  so the dispersion cannot enter it: an envelope run of one, grad_sensitivities' included, now
  says so, and so does the Sacherer solver, which models no dispersion.

### What a run writes and shows

* **A final `.dst` holds the current its particles carry.**  `--write-dst` and the
  backtrack window wrote the particles still in the beam with the full starting
  current, so each came back with too much charge on re-import: 200/183 on a beam
  that lost 17 of 200.  They now write I × N_alive / N_launched, as TraceWin's own
  export after losses does.  A run with no losses writes the same file as before.  A
  run that reads a `.dst` keeps its own current, and now warns whenever that differs
  from the file's, not only for a file that flags its lost particles.

* **The `ε_x growth` card after an envelope run** showed the ratio of the
  geometric emittance, which shrinks with acceleration: 0.24× through an RFQ
  whose normalised emittance grows 2.03×.  It now shows the normalised growth,
  as it already did after a multiparticle run.

### Space charge, matched beams and the Sacherer envelope

* **The matched beam of a periodic cell without RF is now found, for a bunched beam.**  Nothing
  changes a particle's energy with its phase in such a cell (a transport line's FODO or arc), so
  no matched bunch exists, and the matched-beam search handed on wherever it stopped: it did not
  converge (Σ_xx 29 % low on a test FODO, where a change of 1e-15 in the input moved Σ_φφ by
  0.26 deg²), or, with a long bunch, the bunch-length entry dominated its tolerance and let a
  convergence through that was not there.  It now holds the incoming beam's bunch length and
  energy spread there, and solves for the transverse beam, with the incoming beam's betatron
  normal-mode emittances, and for its dispersion, which in a cell with bends comes out as the
  cell's periodic dispersion.  It converges, against a tolerance on the beam it solves for, and
  says so: `longitudinal="held"` and `longitudinal_held` in its result, a warning, and a note in
  the tune footprint's model line.  A search that does not converge now
  warns, and the footprint's model line says so; so does a held beam with a negative eigenvalue,
  which is reported as not converged.  This changes the tune footprint and the matched channel phase
  advance of a bunched beam in a periodic cell without RF: in the arc
  cell of a transfer-line deck σ_x of the matched beam is 36 % larger, and the search
  takes 0.7 s instead of 40 s.  With space charge the held beam is periodic only as far as
  holding the bunch allows: one pass through that cell moves it by 0.4 %.  DC beams and cells
  with RF take the same path as before; what is new for them is the warning when a search does
  not converge, the note in the footprint's model line, and two keys (None) in the results.

* **The space-charge field of a Gaussian beam is right everywhere.**  The field of a 2-D Gaussian
  line charge, which the `"gaussian"` DC kernel and the tune footprint's frozen field use, was
  computed on one side of the beam as the difference of two huge, nearly equal numbers: above 5 %
  asymmetry it was more than 1 % wrong beyond 2.3 σ just past 5 %, 3.2 σ at σ_x/σ_y = 1.1, 7.8 σ
  at 1.5 and 12 σ at 2, and infinite or NaN further out; below 5 % the round-beam field stood in,
  up to 2.5 % off, with a 2.6 % jump at the switch; and across the beam's axes, where the field is
  exactly zero, it carried round-off, from which the footprint took the tunes of rays launched in
  one plane (5.2° to 17.3°, in no order).  It is now evaluated where the formula is accurate, with
  the signs restored by symmetry, and below 1e-4 asymmetry the exact field is expanded about the
  round beam: within 1e-10 of the field wherever that is above 1 % of its peak, 1e-12 of the peak
  everywhere, exactly odd in x and in y.  A footprint ray launched in one plane stays in it; its
  tune in the other plane, its small-amplitude tune there, comes from a shadow copy started with a
  small angle in that plane and now rises with the ray's amplitude; where that small motion is
  unstable (the frozen field's centre is unstable in that plane) the tune is left out and the
  model line counts the rays.  The `"gaussian"` kernel uses the upright Gaussian of the beam's
  projected sizes, an x–y correlation aside, as the manual now says.  This changes tune footprints
  (on a test FODO the largest tune goes from 17.3° to 14.1° and the spread from 13.2° to 9.9°) and
  runs with `dc_kernel="gaussian"`, which no example project selects (a LEBT with it: rms sizes
  +0.13 % and −0.13 %, emittances +0.18 % and −0.06 %, the same transmission).  Nothing else
  reaches the field: a 2907-element linac and a LEBT plus RFQ, run at their saved settings, give
  the same results bit for bit.

* **The Sacherer envelope reads a field map as every other mode does.**  It took a map's
  on-axis B_z times its raw kb, so an error study's or a failure's `voltage_rel`, the map's
  scale and the field file's `Norm` were ignored, and kb = 0 read as kb = 1.  On a DC LEBT
  deck a solenoid map switched off by a failure (`voltage_rel` = −1) left the exit where it
  was, 2.287 mm, and kb = 0 sent it to 4.497 mm; both now give 1.783 mm, where the matrix
  envelope gives 0.798 mm: the rest of that gap is the map's `.scc` compensation profile,
  which the Sacherer does not read.  A 3-D map's B_z came from the middle of its grid instead
  of the axis; a G(z) gradient map focused both planes as a solenoid would, and a 3-D
  quadrupole or dipole map, with no B_z on its axis, was a drift; a 2-D cylindrical solenoid
  map and a `SUPERPOSE_MAP` cluster were drifts.  Now a map's amplitude is its own, B_z is
  read on the axis, a cluster sums its solenoid maps, and a steering-corrector map (a dipole
  field on the axis) is left out, standing alone or in a cluster, as a `STEERER` card is (a
  B_z it holds on the axis focuses as a solenoid's),
  while the correctors of the map or of the cluster together bend the beam by at most
  20 mrad (the largest angle the beam reaches, both planes together) and their quadrupole
  gradient on the axis, as the beam sees it (turned by the beam's Larmor rotation in the
  solenoid's field), focuses it by at most 1/f = 0.0088 /m, the largest value along the map,
  so that each of a corrector's two opposite end lobes counts.  The bound lies where leaving
  the gradient out costs 0.5 % of an rms size: one LEBT deck's first two corrector pairs
  reach it at 2.4 to 2.5 mrad, where their gradient moves the rms sizes by at most 0.50 %, and
  are refused beyond it (left out at their 16 mrad limit, their gradient would move them by up
  to 3.4 %, both of a pair by 4.8 %).  What a corrector's steering does next to a solenoid no
  envelope follows.  A powered map the solver has no model of (a G(z) gradient, a 3-D
  quadrupole whose magnetic centre lies on its grid, a 3-D map whose grid point nearest the
  axis shows a solenoid's field the axis does not hold, or the other way round, a multipole,
  correctors that bend the beam by more than 20 mrad or focus it by more than 0.0088 /m, a
  static electric map) is refused before the run, naming it: run such a line with the matrix
  envelope.  In an error study, a seed whose draws push a corrector past
  either bound (that deck's first two pairs past 2.4 to 2.5 mrad) stops the study.  A 2-D
  Cartesian map is refused too; no mode tracks one.  RF maps stay drifts.  Standalone
  solenoid maps at their nominal settings, their grid centred on the axis, give the same
  results as before.

### Matching and sensitivities

* **The gradient matcher refuses what its model would leave out.**  A foil and a matrix element
  that changes the reference energy have an identity transfer map, so its check passed them as
  no-ops, while its torch model has no scattering and does not follow a matrix element's energy
  change: with a quadrupole after them, 3 MeV protons came out at σ_x 0.608 mm in its model
  where the envelope gives 2.300 mm behind a 600 µg/cm² carbon foil and 0.712 mm behind a matrix
  element that gains 0.5 MeV.  With space charge, a `SPACE_CHARGE_COMP` card passed the same way,
  and a match through a fully compensated line came out at the uncompensated answer (5.144 T/m
  where `least_squares` finds 3.656 T/m).  It now refuses all three, naming the element, and
  points to `least_squares`, which models them; a foil of no thickness, which changes nothing,
  is accepted.

* **The Matching tab's Apply is an undoable edit of the lattice in hand.**  It installed the
  matched copy of the lattice, another object: the undo history was lost, the Apply could not be
  undone, and the Surrogates tab started over, unticking every surrogate, so the next envelope run
  used none of their weights (a half-wave-resonator match deck, with the 11 sets that passed the
  check before the others were retrained: 11 network answers before the Apply, none after).  Apply
  now writes the settings the match's `ADJUST` variables varied into the lattice in hand, and the
  beam fields its `ADJUST_BEAM_*` cards varied into the beam and the Beam tab, as one step: one
  Undo puts back both, Redo applies them again, and the unsaved-changes details list it.  Nothing
  else changes.  The lattice keeps its file, its undo history, the selection and the Surrogates
  tab's rows, Use ticks and registrations (a setting their weights hold that the match changed has
  them refuse, as an inspector edit does); Save matched .dat still writes the matched lattice.  A
  beam field set after the Apply keeps its value through Undo and Redo, and a value typed in the
  Beam tab and not applied stays there.  Apply refuses, changing nothing, when another lattice was
  opened while the match ran, or when the lattice or the beam changed after Match was clicked: an
  element added, removed, moved, renamed or replaced, a value edited, the Beam tab's Apply.  The
  status line says the result no longer holds, and a change made after the result arrived drops
  it.  While an envelope or multiparticle run is going (it walks the elements of the lattice in
  hand), Apply changes nothing and keeps the result (click it again when the run has ended), and
  the Undo and the Redo of an Apply change nothing and say why on the status line.  A run stopped
  with the toolbar's Stop is going until its worker has ended (the `sacherer` envelope solver
  walks on to the end of the lattice).

* **The assistant's `grad_sensitivities` at a chosen element.**  `at_index` takes the beam
  just after that element (0-based, the element included), as its description says.  The
  beam was taken there, but the element's own knob was left out of the ranking, the probe
  was reported one element early, the first element could not be chosen and one past the
  end could.

### Error studies, failure compensation and reliability

* **Field errors on static magnet field maps.**  A `field_rel` draw — the Error Study tab's
  solenoid target draws it — found neither `field` nor `field_rel` on a field map and was skipped
  with a warning, so every solenoid field map ran without its field error (all 8 on a
  half-wave-resonator deck).  On a field map whose fields are all static and magnetic (a
  solenoid's, a quadrupole's, a dipole's) it now adds to `voltage_rel`, so `kb → kb·(1 + δ)`; the
  draws are unchanged (each element is drawn before it is applied), a map with an RF or electric
  channel (a cavity, an unpowered spare) still skips with the warning, the audit says what lands,
  and a `field_rel` and a `voltage_rel` def on the same map are overlapping selectors.

* **Field errors on `SUPERPOSE_MAP` solenoid clusters.**  A `field_rel` draw on a straight
  `SUPERPOSE_MAP` cluster of static magnet maps (a solenoid with its steering correctors) now
  lands in the cluster's own `voltage_rel`, scaling every child; a curved cluster still skips it
  with a warning.

* **A bend is never a failure compensator, and a failed bend is not compensable.**  A bend sat in
  the "magnet" family with the quadrupoles and solenoids, so it was chosen to compensate a
  failed quadrupole through its angle, which moves the reference path (on a test line a ±50 %
  window moved the exit RF phase by ±166°); a failed bend at 90 % on that line (exit orbit +21.6
  mm / +46.3 mrad) was reported recovered by quadrupoles left as they were, or turned to 0 T/m
  (+28.2 mm / +64.9 mrad); and an element with no family fell back to "magnet", so a failed
  steerer got solenoids.  Compensation now stays within the failed element's family: RF
  (cavities, and spares when asked for) or transverse focusing (quadrupoles and solenoids).  A
  failed bend is reported not compensable by re-tuning optics without a match, an element with
  no family gets no compensators, a manual zone keeps to the family too and says what it drops
  (a bend, another family's element), the Reliability Study plans its spares strategy for an RF
  fault only, and its Apply refuses a bend setting from a campaign run before.

* **An unpowered spare cavity is not offered to a failed magnet.**  Zone selection read an
  unpowered field map (ke = kb = 0) as a solenoid and offered it to focusing faults (4
  quadrupole faults of a linac deck); it now reads every label in the Reliability
  Study's vocabulary, where such a map is a spare of the RF family, and a multi-gap cavity is a
  cavity, compensated by cavities.  Spares are still candidates only when asked for.

* **A field-map compensator is re-tuned through the knob that scales its field.**  A static
  solenoid map was re-tuned through its ke and phase, which do nothing to a static field, so the
  compensation left the exit sizes exactly as they were; it is now re-tuned through kb.  A static
  quadrupole or dipole map is no compensator (a dipole map's kb would move the reference path),
  and a cavity map's ke and kb are varied together (one optimiser column when they are equal), so
  its RF electric and magnetic fields keep their ratio.

* **A "neighbouring cells" zone keeps to the failed element's family.**  It took every knob in
  the cells, so a failed quadrupole re-tuned cavities and a failed cavity quadrupoles, and the
  line's first focusing element and everything after the last, which the cell rule leaves out,
  were in no cell; now only the failed element's family is taken, the first cell reaches back to
  the line's start and the last on to its end, and an unpowered spare no longer counts as a
  focusing element of a cell (the cells beside the spares were one element long).

* **The failures CLI compensates the project's beam, and the Failure Study tab's manual zone and
  objectives work.**  The CLI compensated with a default beam (3 MeV at 352.21 MHz) instead of
  the project's, so a failed cavity of one deck came out at 6.72 MeV against its 23.72 and not
  recovered.  The tab's manual strategy passed no names, so it selected nothing, and its
  objectives (the exit energy alone) left a magnet fault's compensation nothing to do while it
  was still reported recovered; it now takes the elements picked in the list (of the failed
  element's family), and the tab and the CLI both take the Reliability Study's objectives (exit
  energy, transmission, emittance growth).

* **The Reliability Study's tuning windows.**  Every compensator got (0, headroom) × a family
  median, its family the leading letters of its name: one median of the signed quadrupole
  gradients (+6.57 T/m on a linac deck) left 81 of 124 quadrupoles' own setting outside
  their window, and one "FMAP" family lumped every field-map cavity type into one nominal, so
  headroom keys naming a cavity type never matched.  The headroom rule is now the RF's, by cavity
  type (a field-map cavity's field map, an RF gap's name pattern), a powered cavity's window
  holds its own setting (one above headroom × its type's median could not return to it), and a
  magnet's window is its own setting × (0.5, 1.5), so it keeps its sign.

* **Fault scoring sees the orbit.**  The criticality rule looked at emittance growth, beam loss,
  exit energy and loss power: in envelope mode, which loses no particle, a bend at 90 % that put
  the beam edge outside the aperture at 327 elements of a transfer line scored non-critical.  A
  run now records the beam edge over the aperture, (|centroid| + 3σ) over an element's
  half-aperture (a rectangular bore's per plane, a circular card's radius), element by element,
  and a fault is critical where that passes the aperture and the baseline's own ratio there
  (`aperture_edge` in the rule; `null` switches it off), so a collimator the beam already fills
  does not mask the rest of the line.  A compensated run is judged with it too, and `faults.csv`
  gives the ratio and its element.

### Surrogates: species, clock and phase

* **A surrogate no longer answers for another species, and a magnet's follows the
  clock.**  A map trained for one species goes to the native element on another beam (a file that
  records no species counts as trained for H⁻, below).  A magnet's map is converted to the
  clock it is asked at.  A cavity with no frequency of its own runs on the clock, and
  answers only on the one it was trained at.  In a file that records no clock, a
  magnet's Δφ–ΔW term shows the clock it was trained on, and it goes native at another
  one.

* **A surrogate file that records no species counts as trained for H⁻.**  It answered for
  any beam, though the Surrogates tab and the CLI trained H⁻ only until files recorded
  their species, and an H⁻ map is not a proton's.  It now answers only for H⁻, in every
  mode and on the gradient matcher's path; loading it says so, and the tab's row reads
  "trained for H-" under another beam.  Nothing is written into the file.

* **The surrogate CLI runs the species its weights are for.**  `compare` and `run-envelope`
  built an H⁻ beam whatever the weights held, so a map for protons never engaged (no NN
  query), and `train` sampled H⁻ only.  All three take `--species`: `compare` and
  `run-envelope` default to the species the weights record (a file that records none
  counts as H⁻) and ask for it when the weights are for several; each weight that cannot
  engage for the beam says so.  `train --species proton` writes to
  `weights/<hash16>_proton/`, the folder the Surrogates tab uses.

* **Older cavity surrogates are checked when loaded.**  New files record the clock of
  their targets.  In a file that does not, a cavity map's longitudinal determinant must
  be 1; one whose targets hold a frequency jump reads f₂/f₁ and is marked stale: its row
  says so, it is never in use and Compare skips it, the status bar's warnings list it,
  and the native cavity tracks until it is retrained.  A factor within 5 % of 1 is not
  detected.

* **Surrogates at a frequency jump.**  With the opt-in fast path, a cavity at another
  frequency than the clock and no `FREQ` card before it kept the old clock through
  the cavity and after it.  Such a traversal now goes to the native element, at its
  cost; after a card the fast path stays on.  A cavity entered out of the surrogate's
  scope now stays native too, instead of restarting part way.  Training computed each
  target at the template's frequency, so for a cavity at another frequency the target
  held the jump's factor (its Δφ–Δφ element too large by f₂/f₁), which the envelope
  applies again.  Targets now use the cavity's own frequency, and a surrogate asked at
  another clock applies the factor itself, as the native element does.  No deck changes
  at nominal settings.  A surrogate of your own whose template was at another frequency
  than its cavity is marked stale when loaded: retrain it.

* **The gradient matcher through a surrogated cavity at another frequency.**  The numpy
  surrogate makes the cavity's jump to its own clock, as the native element does; the
  torch path gave the map alone and went on at the old clock, so its line differed from
  numpy's (the Δφ column by the frequency ratio) and the gradient match refused or
  solved another lattice.  The torch arm now makes the jump, and both torch walks carry
  on at the cavity's clock: torch and numpy agree, and the gradient match gives what
  `least_squares` gives.  With space charge, the half-kick after the cavity is on its
  clock too, and the RF shift of `SET_BEAM_PHASE_ERROR` is read on it.

* **A surrogate placed in the lattice makes its cavity's clock jump** (the compare flows,
  Python API).  It did not carry its element's frequency, so the envelope made no jump
  to the clock of a cavity at another frequency there: σ_φ after such an `NCELLS` or
  field map was off.  It now carries the element's frequency, effective frequency and
  electric channel, and runs as the element does; a magnet's clock does not change.

* **A surrogate placed in the lattice keeps the element's reference energy.**  The
  envelope, matrix mode and the backtracker walk a field map's reference on the
  multiparticle tracker's schedule, but did not recognise a surrogated one, which then
  walked the map's full steps: its exit energy differed from the element's.  The walk,
  its step counts, the cursor it resets and the backtracker's replay are now the
  element's own, and so are, in a multiparticle run, the map's aperture map and
  space-charge compensation table and how the tracker classifies it.

* **A cavity surrogate follows the running phase where its field does.**  At a `p_flag`
  other than 1 a field map's field follows the running phase, so its map depends only on
  the cavity's phase plus the running one, which the surrogate did not take as an input.
  New files record the reference phase they were trained at, and the phase input is
  shifted by how far the phase at the entrance is from it.  The Surrogates tab trains
  each such cavity at the phase it is entered at; the CLI trains at phase 0.  A file
  from before counts as trained at phase 0, which the tab and the CLI used: retrain one
  trained through the Python API at another phase.

* **Which surrogated field maps follow the running phase.**  A `p_flag` 0 or 2 map counted
  as following it when it had an electric channel.  A cavity switched off (`ke` 0), whose
  native map is the same at every phase, then went native off its training phase and was
  refused by the gradient matcher; a map with a static electric channel only was treated
  the same way; and an RF magnetic channel, which does follow the phase, was not shifted.
  It now follows it when an RF channel, electric or magnetic, has a non-zero amplitude.

* **An `NCELLS` P=1 surrogate follows the running phase** (Python API).  Its gaps fire at
  the running phase minus θs, so its map moves with the phase at its entrance, which is
  not an input: off its training phase it answered as at that phase.  Its `theta_s_deg`
  input is now shifted the other way, the native identity M(θs, φ) = M(θs − Δ, φ − Δ);
  with θs not swept it goes native beyond 0.01°, and the gradient matcher refuses it.

* **A cavity surrogate from before the training phase was recorded is checked once.**  It
  counts as trained at phase 0, which the Surrogates tab and the CLI used, so one trained
  through the Python API at another phase, for a cavity whose field follows the running
  phase, took a wrong phase input everywhere.  At its first use after loading, if its
  phase was swept, its map is scanned over the sweep against one native evaluation; when
  phase 0 fits clearly worse than the best fit, it goes native, and the message says
  near which phase it fits (an estimate) or that the best fit is at the edge of the
  sweep.  The phase it was trained at can be written into its `metadata.json` as
  `train_phi_s_deg`.  A file whose phase was not swept cannot be checked.

### Surrogates: when the weights answer

* **A surrogate answers for the element as an error study leaves it.**  It answered with
  the design map whatever the study had done to the element: a `voltage_rel` or a
  `phase_offset` drawn on a surrogated cavity changed nothing in the run.  Each error,
  and the RF shift of `SET_BEAM_PHASE_ERROR`, is now folded into the input it amounts to
  (the phase or θs, `scale` or `ke` and `kb`, the frequency), each identity checked
  against the native element.  When that input was not swept in training, the native
  element tracks instead and the gradient matcher refuses, as it does for what no input
  stands for: an RF shift on a `p_flag` 1 map, `voltage_rel` on an `NCELLS`, a phase
  offset or RF shift under `SET_SYNC_PHASE`.  A `p_flag` 1 map whose calibration train
  mode or a frozen reliability pin keeps goes native, error or not: its weights were
  calibrated afresh at each input.

* **A copy of the lattice decides for itself which surrogates engage.**  The decision is
  pinned on each element for a run, and the pin went with a deep copy, network and all:
  each lattice copy of an error or failure study carried its own copy of the surrogate,
  whose queries no report counted, and a pickled element carried the surrogate with it.
  A copy now decides afresh.  The same element in another object (a reloaded deck, a
  study's copy) gets a view of the weights bound to it, which reads its parameters, so
  an `ADJUST` on the copy is followed, and shares the network and the NN-call count.

* **Surrogate weights from a file answer only within accuracy limits of their native element.**
  Every registered set answered, however far off: with 16 weight sets trained for a
  half-wave-resonator deck at 0 mA the exit σφ came out 13.1 % high and σW 20.7 % low (εx, εy,
  εz 1.1 to 1.5 % high), and a failure study of that deck inherited it.  At their first use
  after a load inside their range (any run, any copy of the lattice), a file's weights are now
  compared with the native element at the input of that use and at 16 points of their training
  range: the largest 2×2-block error must be within 3e-3 (each block over the largest native
  block of its row and column, in units where a 1 m drift reads the same in every plane: in
  degrees and MeV a cavity's Δφ–ΔW term hides its longitudinal focusing), and the determinants
  of the transverse 4×4 and the longitudinal block within 1e-3 of the native map's.  Weights
  that miss go native in envelope and multiparticle runs, the gradient matcher stops with an
  error naming them, and a warning says by how much, once per load.  The Surrogates tab has an
  **Accuracy check** column; its Compare still runs refused weights where they would answer (at
  0 mA), says whether they did, and leaves the one-time check out of its timing.  Of that deck's
  sets, 11 passed (the 8 solenoids and 3 cavities; the line's exit was then within 0.11 % of
  native) and 5 cavities did not; they have been retrained, with the training recipe below.
  Weights built in memory are not checked.  A failure study first runs its
  design lattice natively and with the registered surrogates and uses them only if every exit
  rms size and emittance agrees within 0.5 % (a multiparticle study, or a compensation whose
  cost is a multiparticle run, runs native), and says which in its status line; the Error
  Study tab, a parameter study and a reliability campaign run native and say so.  Backtracking
  through weights their check refused uses the element's exact inverse.

* **A surrogate answers for its element as the element stands.**  The registry recognised an
  element by its settings too, so an error study's seed (an error drawn on the element), a
  parameter scan's point (unless its first value was the element's own) or a deck edited since
  never met the weights, while a match that moved a setting the weights do not take kept them
  answering with the old one.  It now recognises an element by its structure (class, length, field
  file, geometry, aperture, steps, `p_flag`, `ki`, `ka`), and the weights check the element each
  time they are asked: its structure still the one they were built on, their inputs read as the
  element stands (inside their range), the error knobs folded in, the misalignments applied around
  it, and every other setting equal to what they hold within one part in 10⁹ — a pinned sync-phase
  calibration and the field-map integrator among them, a setting the map cannot feel (the phase of
  a static magnet) left out.  The held settings are recorded in training from now on, taken before
  the samples (which are taken with the error knobs at zero, as a query folds them in); an older
  file takes the settings of the element it is first built on.  Otherwise the native element
  answers, and the refusal is counted by reason, per thread too; a compare report lists them.  On
  a half-wave-resonator deck with errors (1 % and 1° on the cavities, 0.5 % on the solenoids, 0.2
  mm offsets, 0.1° tilts), 10 seeds of an envelope error study now take every accepted set's
  answer (16 per seed, once the refused sets were retrained with the recipe below), each seed's
  exit σ and emittance within 0.22 % of the native seed, in 0.23 s against 0.47 s. A compensation
  runs native; a match or a Pareto exploration whose variable moves a setting a surrogate's
  weights hold fixed, or leaves their training range, runs that element native from its first
  evaluation to its last, and warns, and one that mixed the network and the native element says so
  after it; an `NCELLS` set otherwise, or with a pinned calibration, runs native instead of
  answering wrong.  The Parameter Scan runs the design check of the studies (the Sacherer envelope
  runs native), runs an element whose weights hold the scanned setting native at every point, and
  counts the network answers over its points on its status line.  The backtracker inverts exactly
  every study copy the forward run pushed through its native element.  `register-multi` says which
  names run native.

* **Error studies with the envelope.**  `ErrorStudy(mode="envelope")`, and the Error Study tab's
  new **forward** choice (multiparticle stays the default), run each seed's rms envelope from the
  same error draws as the multiparticle study (`env_solver` "matrix" or "sacherer"; an envelope
  loses no particle, and the Results tab's ensemble popup says so instead of showing a 100 %
  transmission).  An envelope study uses the registered surrogates when its design lattice with
  them agrees with the native run (the Sacherer envelope runs native).  It then counts per seed,
  in its own thread, the network's answers and the traversals left to the native element by
  reason, warns when more than 10 % of the traversals the network would answer in a seed fall
  outside its trained range, runs its orbit correction native, runs its first three seeds again
  natively and reports their largest exit difference, and gives each seed's exit values as run
  and, for a seed the network answered as it answered the design lattice, as the native design
  value plus the network's change from its own design value.  A stop ends the design check at the
  next element and skips the native check.  On a half-wave-resonator deck with errors (1 % and 1°
  on the cavities, 0.5 % on the solenoids, 0.2 mm offsets, 0.1° tilts), once the refused sets were
  retrained: 10 seeds took 16 network answers each, every seed's exit σ and emittance within 0.22
  % of the native study's as run and within 0.06 % corrected, the first three run again natively
  within 0.22 %; the study took 9.5 s against 4.8 s natively with its one-time checks, 0.23 s
  against 0.47 s per seed beyond them.  A multiparticle error study runs native, and without a
  registry loaded it leaves the surrogate package (and torch) unloaded.

* **Surrogates in the scan pool's workers.**  A worker process starts with an empty registry, so a
  failure sweep or a reliability campaign run through the pool tracked every point natively while
  the same sweep run serially used the registered weights: on a half-wave-resonator deck a
  four-fault envelope sweep (with the 11 sets that passed the check before the others were
  retrained) took 55 network answers serially and none with two workers, and their exit values
  differed by up to 2.6e-3.  `python -m linac_gen failures` and `reliability run` now take
  `--surrogates DIR[,DIR...]|auto` (a set's folder, a folder of sets named by element, or the
  lattice's own weights folder): the command registers the weights, runs the design check of the
  study's envelope runs and every one-time check still due, and gives each point the weights with
  its metadata (accuracy verdicts, refusals included, held settings), which each worker reads
  once, checks against what the command loaded, and builds on its own parse before the point's
  overrides.  Each row carries the network's answers and the traversals left native
  (`surrogate_calls`, `surrogate_refused`), the same serially and in parallel.  Weights not in a
  folder, or unreadable or changed on disk since, run native in the workers, with a warning;
  multiparticle runs, orbit corrections and compensations run native. A campaign records the
  choice and refuses a resume with another.

* **An edit keeps the Surrogates tab's rows and their Use ticks.**  Every inspector edit and undo
  re-broadcasts the lattice, and the tab cleared its rows and the registry then, unticking every
  surrogate.  It now keeps them for an edit of the lattice in hand (the weights check how their
  element is set each time they are asked), drops, unregisters and names a row whose element
  left the lattice, was renamed or changed its structure, and starts over for another lattice or
  another beam species.  An old file's held settings stay those of the element as it was loaded.

* **Backtracking through surrogate weights.**  With multiparticle surrogates on, the backtracker
  took the linear inverse of every field map with registered weights, also where the forward run
  had pushed it natively (with the fast path off, the default, every push is native), under a
  caveat that the element was "driven by a surrogate": on a half-wave-resonator deck the
  reconstructed entrance was off by up to 2.8 mm, 4.3 mrad, 9° and 22 keV.  The forward run now
  records on the beam what the weights pushed, and the backtracker undoes each push as it was
  made, exactly (the weights' own matrix slices inverted, the native element's steps by its exact
  inverse), whatever the fast-path box says when it runs: that deck closes to 1e-12 (1.6e-11° in
  phase) with the fast path off and on, also after a marker is added or a BPM deleted.  The record
  also holds each element's settings (its structure among them, such as its step count and
  aperture), its step schedule and the reference at its entrance, for a traversal the native
  element made alone as for one through the weights; after an edit that changes one of them (the
  element's own settings, the step density, an edit upstream that moves its entrance, the beam's
  entrance energy) the element is undone as the weights would push it now, with a caveat that says
  what changed, unless the native element pushes it both in the run and now.  So is a beam whose
  record does not cover the lattice walked: one read from a file, with a caveat wherever the
  weights push the element now or, with the fast path off, would push it with the fast path on;
  one tracked through another lattice, with a caveat wherever weights stand for the element now or
  pushed an element of its name in the run.

* **A retrain after a Save.**  Retraining an engaged element after saving the lattice left the
  old weights registered and in use while its row showed its Use box unticked; the fresh weights
  now take the registration.

### Surrogates: training

* **The Surrogates tab trains on the project's beam.**  It used H⁻ at 2.12 MeV and
  162.5 MHz whatever the project, including for the energy range it detects.  It now
  takes the project's species, energy and clock, and for a `.dst` source those of the
  file, as a multiparticle run does; a beam it cannot build stops the training with a
  message.  Proton and deuteron weights go in their own folder, named by the species
  trained, and the tab reads the folder of the beam's species again when that changes,
  so a retrain cannot overwrite H⁻ weights.  H⁻ keeps the folder it had.

* **Surrogate training fits a map's real entries.**  Each of the 36 outputs was normalised by its
  own spread over the training data, so the zero couplings of a symmetric cavity, which hold only
  round-off (spread about 1e-15 to 2e-12), weighed as much as a real entry: on a
  half-wave-resonator cavity they held 99.8 % of the validation error.  With a constant learning
  rate and the epoch kept by validation MAPE, more epochs (up to 3000) and a wider or deeper
  network did not bring the refused cavity weights within the accuracy limits at 5000 samples, and
  four times the samples only just (determinant error 9.7e-4 against 1e-3).  An output's spread is
  now floored at 1e-9, the learning rate falls along a half cosine to 1 % of its starting value at
  the last epoch, and the epoch kept is the one with the lowest validation error (the mean squared
  error of the normalised outputs).  At the same 5000 samples and 128×128 network over 1000
  epochs, five refused half-wave-resonator cavity sets and two buncher sets retrained this way
  pass the check with a margin of at least 1.9 (block error 2.6e-4 to 1.3e-3 against 3e-3,
  determinant error 7e-5 to 5.3e-4 against 1e-3).  The val MAPE a training reports is over the
  outputs that vary; the training window plots the val MAPE of the epoch kept; the Surrogates
  tab's **Train all FieldMap\*** defaults to 1000 epochs; a training of no epoch is refused; a
  weights file records how it was trained (`train_recipe`) and what its element was when the
  training started (`train_structure`, the phase reference `p_flag` among it); and
  `helix_commit_sha` is the commit of the HELIX checkout that trained it, where it was the HEAD of
  whatever repository the working directory was in.  Weight files load and answer as before: the
  accuracy verdicts and the 0 mA envelopes of 72 weight sets on 5 decks are bit-identical.

## Other changes

### Added

* **The `.dst` files TraceWin writes with seven values per particle** (seen
  from 2.27).  They failed to load with a file-size error.  The seventh value
  flags a lost particle, which stays in the file: the reader keeps the particles
  still in the beam and scales the current to them, as TraceWin's own six-value
  export does after losses.  Six-value files give the same particles as before.

### Fixed

* **The gradient matcher follows `FREQ` and the energy cards** (`; HELIX_SET_ENERGY`, `;
  HELIX_SHIFT_ENERGY`), with and without space charge, where it refused them, and
  `grad_sensitivities` does too.  Its results agree with `least_squares` across each card.

* **Warnings now reach the GUI.**  Warnings from the engine went only to the
  terminal, such as a loaded `.dst` whose current differs from the beam's.  A
  **N warnings** button now appears in the status bar and stays until cleared, with
  the list behind it.  The terminal still gets them.

* **The manual's figures for the projected emittance on `bend_line.dat`** (×93 in
  envelope mode, ×53 with macroparticles) could not be reproduced.  Both modes give
  ×10.7, which the manual now says with the beam it holds for, and a test keeps it right.

* **The Results tab's field-map tile was empty when a deck's maps sit in `SUPERPOSE_MAP`
  clusters.**  It took only plain maps, so a deck with a solenoid and its two correctors
  superposed, or a curved dipole, showed "(no field-map elements)".  It now lists every map of a
  cluster, unpowered correctors too, by file name and strength (`[1] SUPERP_001 › FMAP_002
  (FieldMap3D) — corrector_h · Kb 0`).  It plots the file's values, and its info line gives the
  factor a run multiplies them by. The cavity-TTF window had the same gap and now lists powered
  cavity maps inside a cluster.

* **The test suite, with MAD-X (cpymad) installed,** no longer leaves a
  `checkpoint_restart.dat` in the directory it runs from: MAD-X `TRACK` writes one
  into its working directory, and the tests that run it now give it one of their own.

* **The MAD-X export no longer stops with `math domain error`.**  A linearised element whose
  energy change takes the reference to zero energy or below stopped the export with
  `math domain error` or `p_local/p0 must be positive`.  That covers an explicit matrix
  element's gain or energy offset, the orbit after a cavity, and re-exporting a MAD-X deck
  whose `kick6` loses the reference.  It is now a marker and a drift with one warning, or a
  refusal that says why with `on_unsupported="error"`.  An energy card that cannot apply at
  the energy the export stays at (a shift down after a shift up) stopped the export too;
  it is now a comment, like the others.  When the export loses the reference after an
  energy card it writes as a comment, the warning now names the card, but only if the
  deck itself gets past that point.  Otherwise it names the cause: a curved cluster's
  path that cannot be traced, a negative gain, or the deck's RF.

* **The linearised MAD-X export warns of a misalignment.**  A tilted or offset field map was
  written as a `MATRIX` of the map as aligned, with no warning, though matrix mode tilts the
  map (a 20° tilted quadrupole map: x–y coupling 0.198 in matrix mode, none in the export).
  It is still written aligned, now with the warning every other element gives.  A curved
  field-map cluster moves its own maps, so its `MATRIX` keeps its misalignment and is not
  warned of.

* **A stale or species-less surrogate file says so every time it is loaded.**  Python shows
  a warning once for each line it comes from, and every load comes from the same line, so
  only a session's first load of a stale file, or of one that records no species or an old
  mass, reached the status bar: after **Clear**, loading the project again showed nothing.
  Each load of a project, or of another species' folder, now says it once; an edit of the
  lattice, which reloads the files, does not repeat it.  Warning filters still apply.

* **Tests that need a module or data that a distribution of HELIX leaves out skip when it is
  not there.**  Five test cases of the phase-error card and of the phase advance across a change
  of clock imported an analysis module that not every distribution includes, and twelve tests of
  the beam phase advance and of the orbit response read example decks that not every
  distribution carries; they failed with ModuleNotFoundError or FileNotFoundError where those
  are missing, and now skip, as the other tests of that module and that data do.  A test in
  `tests/scripts` keeps the module guards in place.

* **The tests that read or copy a text file HELIX wrote name its encoding:** UTF-8 for MAD-X
  and project files, latin-1 for TraceWin decks (the deck encoding).  They used the platform's
  default, which on Windows is cp1252: 29 of them failed there on non-ASCII text, the "Bρ" of
  the MAD-X export's header among it.  A test in `tests/scripts` keeps it so.

* **Two tests of matching across a card that sets the reference no longer fail at random.**
  They compared two runs of the matched-beam search for a bunched beam in a FODO without RF,
  where no matched bunch exists: the search stopped unconverged, and a change of 1e-15 in its
  input moved its answer by 0.26, so the last bits of the session decided the outcome.  They
  now compare the period's map and the matched beam with its bunch length held, which are well
  defined.

* **grad_sensitivities takes every element a magnet line carries.**  A marker (a beam position
  monitor among them), an aperture, a space-charge compensation or grid card, a steerer, a thin
  lens, a multipole, a matrix element or a foil stopped it with an error from the torch matrix
  path, so a deck with diagnostic markers failed at its first one; and an RFQ cell got past its
  refusal, whose name test was case-sensitive, to the same error.  It now walks the zero-current
  envelope: the knobs through the differentiable matrices, an element with no knob as the
  constant map the other modes use, a foil as the envelope takes it (its scattering added to the
  beam, its mean energy loss taken off the reference), and a matrix element's change of the
  reference energy.  Every other class is refused by name (field maps, RF gaps, multi-gap
  cavities, RFQ cells and any class it does not know, a subclass of a magnet among them), and so
  is a foil or matrix element that would leave the beam with no energy.  A line of the types it
  took before gives the same answers, bit for bit.  Of the example projects, every one that
  stopped with an error now answers (a FODO cell with markers and one with correctors among
  them), and every one it answered before gives the same numbers.  Its refusal no longer names a
  machine.  A continuous beam no longer starts from the bunch settings its project still holds
  (the Beam tab greys them out without clearing them), where the envelope seeds no
  longitudinal block: behind a bend its σx and σy came out wrong and the ranking flipped
  (5.02 mm against the envelope's 1.88 at 30 keV); no example project holds such settings.

* **The fast path's count on a 1-D field map.**  With the multiparticle fast path on, a 1-D field
  map with weights counted one network answer per traversal although the fast path cannot push
  it and the native element did; it now counts none, and in such a run the weights of a 1-D map
  are not asked at all (no refusal is counted for them and their accuracy check does not run).

* **The training window no longer holds a training back.**  It painted every epoch, a full redraw
  of about 0.1 s that the training waited for: 1000 epochs took 122 s in the batch trainer and
  100 s in a single training, against 15 s without the window.  A run of more than 200 epochs
  now paints at most four times a second and always its last epoch (26 s and 21 s); a shorter
  one still paints each.  In the batch trainer, whose window takes progress events from a queue,
  an event that arrived while the window painted was painted inside that paint, one level
  deeper per waiting event, until Python ran out of stack (a RecursionError after about 40 s
  of data generation in a probe at the trainer's event rate); such an event is now recorded and
  painted after.  The manual's data-generation timings are measured again: 5000 samples of a
  MEBT buncher take 41 s on one worker and 12 to 14 s on 4 to 12, and for a few hundred samples
  one worker is fastest.

## Retired

* **`VaneRFQ`, and the Lattice tab's "RfqCell → VaneRFQ" button.**  The
  whole-RFQ element the button made from a `.vane` file does not
  reproduce an RFQ: its multiparticle push never advances the particle
  phase (no phase slip), and its envelope matrix holds the entry velocity
  with no energy gain.  On a 4.4 m RFQ to 2.1 MeV it transmitted 57 %
  where the RFQ_CELL chain transmits 100 %.  The button is gone;
  constructing a `VaneRFQ` in a script still works but emits a
  `VaneRFQRetiredWarning`.  No deck or project is affected: the parser
  never made one, and saving a lattice that held one wrote the RFQ cells
  back.  RFQs are tracked as RfqCell chains: multiparticle runs use the
  time-domain solver by default, which takes the vanes from the deck's
  `RFQ_GEOM` `.vane` file (or builds them from the cards); envelope
  runs and backtracking use the per-cell model.  The recorded Lattice tab
  tutorial (episode 3) still shows the button until it is re-recorded.
