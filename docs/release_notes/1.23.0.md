# HELIX 1.23.0 — consistent stepping through field maps and drifts

Released 2026-10-30.  The steps left after the last space-charge
interval of a field map now sample the field where the beam is; those
of field maps and drifts get their own space-charge kick; envelope,
matrix and backtracking runs advance the reference particle through
field maps exactly as multiparticle runs do.  Results change; the
sections below say where and by how much.

## Changes that can change results

A field map is walked in space-charge intervals: each interval is a run
of half steps, one space-charge kick, and another run of half steps.  When
a map's integration steps are not a whole number of intervals, the steps
left after the last interval were handled wrongly in two ways, now fixed.
A lattice whose field maps all divide into whole intervals gives the same
results to the bit.

* **Where the last steps sample the field** (multiparticle runs, the
  exact backtracker, the `VaneRFQ` element).  The leftover steps sampled
  the field about one map length further on, outside the map, so they
  were tracked as a drift without the field.  They now sample it where
  the beam is.  The exact backtracker repeated the same position and now
  records the position of each step it replays.  A `VaneRFQ` (the
  whole-RFQ element made by the Lattice tab's swap of the RFQ cells for a
  `.vane` file) ran its last steps, less than one interval of the RFQ
  exit, as a drift without RF; they now go through the cells.

* **Where envelope slices sample the field** (envelope runs with space
  charge).  When the half intervals and the last slice of a map used
  sub-steps of different lengths (an odd number of steps per interval),
  the last slices sampled the field from the wrong position.  Each map now
  carries its position from one slice to the next.  Envelope runs without
  space charge and matrix runs are unchanged.

* **Space charge over the last steps** (multiparticle and envelope runs,
  both backtrackers).  The leftover steps had no space-charge kick.  They
  now form a short last interval with its own kick over its own length,
  so the kicks cover the whole map, and are walked in half steps like the
  rest of the map (runs without space charge change too, at the level of
  the integration error).  RFQ cells tracked cell by cell are included:
  a cell whose steps do not divide into whole intervals gets one more,
  short kick.

* **Space charge over a drift's leftover sub-steps** (multiparticle runs,
  both backtrackers, the differentiable step tracker).  The same gap
  existed in drifts: when the integration steps per metre are neither
  equal to nor twice the space-charge steps per metre (150/50, 100/30, …),
  the sub-steps after a drift's last interval ran without a kick.  They
  now form a short last interval with its own kick.  Runs at 1:1 or 2:1,
  the default 100/50 among them, give the same results to the bit.

* **Envelope space-charge steps in RFQ cells** (envelope runs with space
  charge through `RFQ_CELL`, `VaneRFQ` or `NCELLS` elements).  The
  envelope's space-charge walk computed its own step counts without the
  minimum step counts of these elements, so its kicks fell elsewhere than
  a multiparticle run's.  It now uses the same counts.  Field maps are
  unchanged to the bit.  The bunched envelope through an RFQ does not
  converge as the space-charge kicks are refined; read RFQ beam quality
  from multiparticle runs (manual: *The envelope through an RFQ*).

* **Reference particle through field maps in envelope, matrix and
  backtracking runs** (`FIELD_MAP` elements and straight `SUPERPOSE_MAP`
  clusters; RFQ cells, `NCELLS` and curved clusters keep theirs).  These
  modes advanced the reference with the map's own full steps;
  multiparticle runs advance it in half steps.  The reference now takes
  the multiparticle run's own states, so through these maps its energy,
  phase and frequency are the same in every mode.  Also affected: the
  phase-advance and dispersion analyses, the energy gain the MAD-X writer
  emits as `kick6` for a linearised field map, and the linear
  backtracker.  The dispersion walk and the per-cell coupled phase
  advance now also start each field map from a clean state, as the matrix
  mode does: on a lattice that had just been run at another energy they
  used the cavity phase calibration of that run.  The dispersion walk
  now also advances the RF phase through drifts.  A map with a frequency
  error (`frequency_offset`) now leaves an envelope run at its effective
  frequency, as in multiparticle runs; the envelope used to carry the
  nominal frequency there and warn.  Unchanged: the matrix mode and the
  analysis walks still ignore `FREQ` cards.

How large the changes are, on a superconducting linac at 100 integration
steps and 50 space-charge kicks per metre, where cavity maps of 240, 250
and 300 mm leave 6–10 mm after the last interval (5 mA, 10 000
particles):

* field sampling alone: the reference energy at the linac end is lower by
  0.28 keV in 800 MeV; emittances and rms beam sizes change by at most
  0.03 %;
* with the space-charge kicks: rms beam sizes change by up to 0.6 % in
  the low-energy section and by about 0.3 % at the end of the line
  (envelope), and the longitudinal emittance at the end by 0.2–0.3 %
  (multiparticle), less than the difference between two random seeds.
  Transmission changes by at most one particle in 10 000;
* drifts: nothing at 100/50.  At 100/30, where 2.7 % of the drift length
  had no kick, rms beam sizes change by up to 3.4 % in the low-energy
  section and transmission by 0.17 %; results at 100/30 now differ from
  those at 100/50 by 0.01–0.03 % on average along the line, against
  0.45–0.69 % before;
* envelope reference: the envelope's energy at the linac end rises by
  0.29 MeV, to the multiparticle value (it was 0.29 MeV, 4e-4, below);
  rms beam sizes change by at most 0.3 %, matching (BMAG) by at most
  2e-5.

## Other changes

* **`backtrack --validate` compares the same particles.**  The check
  compared the sigmas of the particles that survive the forward re-track
  with those of the whole supplied exit beam, so a few particles lost at
  an aperture made a round trip that closes exactly look like a failure
  of several per cent, and the message blamed the linear field-map
  inverse whichever inverse was used.  The sigmas are now taken over the
  survivors on both sides; the particles the re-track loses are counted
  on their own and fail the check above 5 % of the exit beam; a
  per-particle residual is printed for information; the message names
  what failed and the field-map inverse used.

* **Run time.**  Envelope and matrix runs through field maps take
  6–22 % longer when repeated in a session, for the reference's
  half-step walk (see above); the first run of a session takes about as
  long as before (between 10 % less and 4 % more), because the walk's
  field values are interpolated in one call per map.
